How percentages work

A percentage is a fraction out of 100. The word comes from the Latin “per centum”, meaning “by the hundred”, so 25% means 25 out of every 100, or 0.25 as a decimal. Percentages make it easy to compare amounts of different sizes, which is why they appear in discounts, interest rates, exam marks, taxes and statistics.

Everyday uses

  • Discounts: a jacket costs 2,500 with 30% off. Use the last calculator with 30 and 2500 and read the “Subtract” line: 1,750.
  • Tax and tips: adding a 5% service charge to a bill of 1,200 gives 1,260.
  • Marks: scoring 42 out of 60 is 70%, found with “X is what % of Y”.
  • Growth: if your savings grew from 8,000 to 9,200, the percentage change is +15%.

Percentage increase vs percentage points

Do not confuse a percentage change with a change in percentage points. If an interest rate rises from 4% to 6%, it has gone up by 2 percentage points, but it has increased by 50% in relative terms. Always check which one a statement means.

Remember: a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease does not bring you back to the start. 100 becomes 150, and 50% of 150 is 75, so you end up at 75.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find the original price before a discount?

Divide the discounted price by (1 − discount ÷ 100). For example, a price of 1,750 after a 30% discount came from 1,750 ÷ 0.70 = 2,500.

Can a percentage be more than 100%?

Yes. 150% means one and a half times the original amount. Growth figures often exceed 100%.

Why does the percentage change need a starting value that is not zero?

Percentage change compares the difference with the starting value. If the starting value is zero, the comparison is undefined.
