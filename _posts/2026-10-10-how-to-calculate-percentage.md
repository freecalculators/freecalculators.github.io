A percentage is a number out of 100. The word comes from the Latin "per centum", meaning "by the hundred", so 25% means 25 out of every 100, or 0.25 as a decimal. Percentages let you compare things of different sizes, which is why you meet them in discounts, marks, interest rates and statistics. Here are the five calculations you will use most often.

## 1. Find a percentage of a number

**Formula:** number × percentage ÷ 100

*Example:* What is 15% of 240?

240 × 15 ÷ 100 = **36**

## 2. Find what percent one number is of another

**Formula:** part ÷ whole × 100

*Example:* You scored 42 out of 60. What is your percentage?

42 ÷ 60 × 100 = **70%**

## 3. Calculate percentage change

**Formula:** (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100

*Example:* A price rises from 80 to 100.

(100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = **25% increase**

If the result is negative, it is a decrease. Always divide by the **old** value, not the new one.

## 4. Add or subtract a percentage

- **Add:** number × (1 + percentage ÷ 100)
- **Subtract:** number × (1 − percentage ÷ 100)

*Example 1:* Add 5% service charge to a bill of ₹1,200.

1,200 × 1.05 = **₹1,260**

*Example 2:* A jacket costs ₹2,500 and is 30% off.

2,500 × 0.70 = **₹1,750**

## 5. Find the original price before a discount

**Formula:** final price ÷ (1 − discount ÷ 100)

*Example:* After a 30% discount the price is ₹1,750. What was the original price?

1,750 ÷ 0.70 = **₹2,500**

## Quick mental tricks

- **10%:** move the decimal point one place left. 10% of 450 is 45.
- **5%:** find 10% and halve it. 5% of 450 is 22.5.
- **1%:** move the decimal point two places left. 1% of 450 is 4.5.
- **15%:** add 10% and 5%. 15% of 240 is 24 + 12 = 36.
- **50%:** divide by 2. **25%:** divide by 4.

## A common mistake

A 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease does not bring you back to where you started. Take 100: a 50% increase gives 150, and a 50% decrease of 150 is 75. The second percentage is taken from a bigger number, so the two do not cancel out.

## Percentage points versus percent

If an interest rate goes from 4% to 6%, it has risen by **2 percentage points**, but by **50 percent** in relative terms (2 ÷ 4 × 100). News reports mix these up often, so check which one is meant.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a percentage be more than 100%?**
Yes. 150% means one and a half times the original amount. Growth figures often go above 100%.

**How do I turn a percentage into a decimal?**
Divide by 100. So 35% becomes 0.35, and 7% becomes 0.07.

**How do I turn a decimal or fraction into a percentage?**
Multiply by 100. So 0.4 is 40%, and 3/4 is 0.75, which is 75%.
