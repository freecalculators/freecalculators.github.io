EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the fixed amount you pay your lender every month until a loan is fully repaid. Every EMI contains two parts: a portion that repays the loan itself (the principal) and a portion that pays the lender's interest. Knowing how it is calculated helps you compare offers and choose a loan you can actually afford.

## The EMI formula

Most banks use the reducing balance method. The formula is:

```
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)
```

- **P** is the loan amount (principal).
- **r** is the monthly interest rate. Divide the yearly rate by 12, then by 100. For 9% a year, r = 9 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0075.
- **n** is the number of monthly instalments. A 5-year loan has n = 60.

## Worked example

Suppose you borrow ₹5,00,000 at 9% a year for 5 years.

1. P = 5,00,000
2. r = 9 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0075
3. n = 5 × 12 = 60
4. (1 + r)^n = 1.0075^60 ≈ 1.5657
5. EMI = 5,00,000 × 0.0075 × 1.5657 ÷ (1.5657 − 1) ≈ **₹10,379 per month**

Over 60 months you would pay about ₹6,22,751 in total, so the interest cost is roughly ₹1,22,751.

You can check this yourself. Open the [scientific calculator]({{ '/' | relative_url }}#calculator) and type:

```
500000×0.0075×(1.0075)^60÷((1.0075)^60−1)
```

Press equals and you should see a value close to 10379.18.

## How tenure changes your EMI

A longer tenure lowers the monthly EMI but increases the total interest. Here is a ₹10,00,000 loan at 9% a year:

| Tenure | Monthly EMI | Total paid | Total interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 years | ₹20,758 | ₹12,45,501 | ₹2,45,501 |
| 10 years | ₹12,668 | ₹15,20,109 | ₹5,20,109 |
| 15 years | ₹10,143 | ₹18,25,680 | ₹8,25,680 |
| 20 years | ₹8,997 | ₹21,59,342 | ₹11,59,342 |

Stretching the loan from 5 to 20 years cuts the EMI by more than half, but you pay almost five times as much interest. A good approach is to pick the shortest tenure whose EMI still fits comfortably in your budget.

## How the interest rate matters

Even one percentage point makes a real difference on a long loan. For a ₹20,00,000 loan over 20 years:

- at 8% the EMI is about ₹16,729,
- at 9% it is about ₹17,995,
- at 10% it is about ₹19,300.

Each extra 1% adds roughly ₹1,300 to the monthly EMI and around ₹3 lakh to the total interest over the full term.

## Tips to keep EMI under control

- **Compare the total cost, not just the EMI.** A low EMI on a very long loan can cost far more overall.
- **Make part-prepayments** when you have spare money. They reduce the principal, so future interest falls.
- **Keep EMIs below about 40% of your monthly income** so you still have room for emergencies. This is a common rule of thumb, not a strict limit.
- **Check processing fees and other charges.** The advertised rate is not always the full cost of a loan.
- **Ask whether the rate is fixed or floating.** A floating rate can change your EMI or your tenure later.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is EMI calculated on the reducing balance or a flat rate?**
Banks normally use the reducing balance method shown above. Some lenders advertise a flat rate, which looks lower but is usually more expensive in real terms.

**Why is more of my early EMI interest?**
Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is highest at the start. As you repay principal, the interest part shrinks and the principal part grows.

**Can I use this formula for any loan?**
Yes. It works for home, car and personal loans that use monthly instalments and the reducing balance method. Your lender's exact figure can differ slightly because of rounding and the date of the first payment.
